About

I hold an MSc in Astrophysics & Cosmology from the University of Padova, where I worked with Prof. Giulia Rodighiero on galaxy mergers in deep JWST fields. For my thesis I built a Python pipeline that searches the ~500,000 sources of the ASTRODEEP-JWST catalogs for close galaxy pairs and measures the major-merger pair fraction out to z ~ 10.

Since graduating I have kept that project going: extending it to the new COSMOS2025 and CEERS catalogs, testing it against the Santa Cruz semi-analytic model, and turning it into a first-author paper. I also collaborate remotely on JWST spectroscopy of high-redshift sources in the PRIMER-COSMOS field.

I'm looking for a PhD position in extragalactic astrophysics, ideally on a project that combines JWST data with careful statistics or machine learning.

Research

My main project: a census of merging galaxy pairs in the deepest JWST images, built from raw catalogs to final statistics.

MSc thesis → first-author paper in preparation

How often did galaxies collide in the early Universe?

Mergers can trigger bursts of star formation and help build massive galaxies, but counting them at high redshift is hard: with only photometric redshifts, a true pair is easily confused with two unrelated galaxies that just happen to line up on the sky. I built a pipeline that turns deep JWST catalogs into a probabilistic census of close pairs, back to when the Universe was about 500 million years old.

How the pipeline works

  1. Data

    Seven ASTRODEEP-JWST fields (CEERS, Abell 2744, NGDEEP, PRIMER-COSMOS, PRIMER-UDS, JADES-GN, JADES-GS), later cross-checked with COSMOS2025 and CEERS DR1.

  2. Clean sample

    Mass-complete galaxies with 8 < log M*/M☉ < 10, split into red and blue populations by NIRCam colour.

  3. Eyes on every galaxy

    Multi-band NIRCam cutouts for every source, checked one by one to remove artefacts and spurious detections.

  4. Pair search

    Companions at 10–50 kpc with mass ratio ≥ 1:4, each pair weighted by its photometric-redshift probability.

  5. Uncertainties

    Bootstrap resampling, plus cross-matching against independent catalogs to catch contamination.

  6. Theory test

    Comparison with pairs drawn from the Santa Cruz semi-analytic model (SC-SAM).

Lesson learned

A sharp spike in the pair fraction at z ≈ 8–9 turned out to be spurious sources, not real mergers. At the highest redshifts, catalog reliability matters as much as the statistics.

Outreach & Astro-Tourism

My vision for astro-tourism brings together observational astronomy and my hands-on experience in Rome's hospitality sector, where welcoming people from every culture taught me the power of human connection. I want to extend that connection to the night sky.

Building on a proposal I wrote for revitalising astronomical infrastructure, my goal is to turn historic Silk Road caravanserais into “hotel-observatories”: dark-sky camps in pristine places like the Lut Desert that honour the legacy of ancient observatories such as Maragheh, and turn science outreach into an unforgettable journey.
